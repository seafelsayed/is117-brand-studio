Research question and method

How should Common Canvas present a plain white T-shirt to college students seeking personal style without pressure, and which product information and design choices would help them make an informed choice?

We compare three retailers selling comparable basic tees. The category is relevant to the existing product; these are not proven campus competitors. AI retrieved the original product-page text on November 16, 2026 and checked the observations below against it. This is desk research, not interviews, a visual audit of retailer layouts, or evidence of shopper behavior. Retailer manufacturing and certification statements are attributed claims, not independently audited facts. Dynamic pages and inventory can change.

Research precedes any new product feature in this checkpoint. The existing storefront was built earlier; the observations below inform proposed refinements, not a claim that this research caused the earlier implementation.

Three sites: source → finding → decision → planned check

F1 — UNIQLO: an adaptable basic

Original source S1: SUPIMA Cotton T-Shirt. Accessed November 16, 2026. Locate Description / Details and Materials / Care. The retrieved URL currently describes product IDs 489931, 482297 and 475357; the selected color was gray with white among the options.

Evidence: The description presents the tee as suitable alone or layered and supplies fabric and care information.

Finding / interpretation: A basic tee can be explained through possible uses rather than an elaborate story. This does not establish that students prefer it.

Proposed decision D1: Retain the existing three optional styling ideas and keep the size action clear. Treat the tips as possibilities, not rules; do not copy UNIQLO's material claims onto our demo shirt.

Planned check C1 — not run: Ask a classmate to find one styling idea and the size action, then explain whether the page offers freedom or prescribes an outfit. Record the task, response, confusion, and any revision. If the ideas sound compulsory or the action cannot be found, revise wording or hierarchy and retry.

F2 — Everlane: context for evaluation

Original source S2: The Essential Organic Crew, White. Accessed November 16, 2026. Locate the model note, Size Guide, and Certifications.

Evidence: The page gives model height and worn size, offers garment/body measurement guidance, and identifies OCS100 in its certification section.

Finding / interpretation: Specific product context gives a visitor something concrete to evaluate. The page alone does not prove lower uncertainty or environmental performance.

Proposed decision D2: Keep known demo details and unknown real-world specifications distinct. A future fit guide must wait for actual garment measurements. Do not invent certifications, fit guarantees, or material facts.

Planned check C2 — not run: Ask a reviewer which shirt facts are known and whether an actual fit is guaranteed. Pass if they recognize the fictional product boundary and no fit guarantee. If not, clarify the disclosure and repeat the task. Obtaining real measurements is a separate prerequisite before building a fit guide.

F3 — Buck Mason: a provenance position

Original source S3: White Slub Curved Hem Tee. Accessed November 16, 2026 (retrieval service reports its page crawl as November 15). Locate the product introduction and Size/Fit Guide.

Evidence: The retailer describes USA-grown cotton and manufacture at its Mohnton, Pennsylvania mill, and provides a size/fit guide.

Finding / interpretation: A plain tee can be distinguished by a manufacturing story. That is a positioning option, not proof that provenance causes purchases.

Proposed decision D3: Compare a craftsmanship position with the existing style-and-belonging position below. Recommend style-and-belonging because Common Canvas has no verified supply-chain facts to support a premium manufacturing promise.

Planned check C3 — not run: Have a reviewer trace every material, origin, price, and quality claim on the demo. Pass if facts have project evidence or are explicitly fictional, and no retailer claim is presented as ours. Remove or qualify any unsupported claim, then request a retry.

Comparison: UNIQLO supplies use cases; Everlane supplies evaluation context; Buck Mason supplies provenance. Together these suggest optional inspiration plus clear information, while showing why a manufacturing-led promise would require evidence we do not have.

Two competing business positions

A — Personal style without pressure: recommended

Audience: college students dressing for class who want a flexible outfit starting point. Promise: a simple starting point for expressing your style, without pressure to fit in. Differentiate through welcoming language and optional styling help. Risk: vague belonging language may not explain a concrete benefit. D1 supplies useful examples; C1 tests the intended meaning.

B — Traceable craftsmanship: alternative

Audience hypothesis: shoppers who prioritize documented materials, origin, and construction. Promise would depend on traceable product facts. S3 shows this approach exists; S2 shows the kind of detail a visitor can inspect. Risk: Common Canvas cannot currently substantiate that promise. Investigate suppliers and measurements before considering it; do not build an ethical or premium halo from competitor claims.

Chosen working audience: retain A from the prior Brand Studio agreement. This is the AI recommendation for the group proposal, not a recorded vote by Varnit. The earlier owner decision in issue #5 also retained the student audience over an everyday-uniform alternative. The group still needs to discuss the new craftsmanship alternative. Competitor pages do not validate student demand, willingness to pay, or belonging concerns.

Two persona hypotheses — assumptions

P1 — Style explorer before class

Situation: choosing an outfit for campus. Goal: feel put together while keeping personal expression. Possible concern: feeling judged. Page need: optional ideas and a clear next action. These are inherited audience assumptions, not an interview or an actual customer's statement. C1 can test whether our message is understood, but not establish demand for a business.

P2 — Detail-checking basic shopper

Situation: comparing plain tees before choosing. Goal: understand fit and product facts. Possible concern: vague quality claims. Page need: clear specifications and disclosure of what is unknown. This contrasting persona is an AI-generated hypothesis inspired by S2 and S3, not an observed person. C2 and C3 test information clarity; real customer research is needed to learn whether this is a priority.

No simulated customer quotes or invented interview results are used. Open research question: do students value optional styling inspiration enough to distinguish this business from a generic tee store?

Design, brand character, and honest persuasion

Reuse the existing postmodernist Brand Studio direction: rounded forms, cream and dark green, warm accents, Georgia headings and Arial body text. Preserve the original SVG wordmark and shared styles. Expressiveness should support a readable hierarchy and a visible size action. This is our design direction, not an inferred visual description of the three retailers.

Brand character: Everyperson: welcoming and approachable; calm, casual, encouraging voice. The established headline “Your style. Your pace.” invites a voluntary choice. Avoid implying that every student shares one identity.

  • Liking: retain the welcoming no-dress-code message. Intended effect: less social pressure. This effect is unmeasured; check its interpretation through C1.
  • Reciprocity: offer styling ideas freely, without registration or purchase. Their usefulness is a hypothesis; a visitor owes nothing in return.
  • Truthful boundaries: the existing $28 is a fictional demo price. The temporary demo bag does not accept orders, payments, or personal information. No testimonials, urgency, stock scarcity, sustainability claims, or manufacturing promises will be invented. C2/C3 check whether these boundaries are clear.

Before any new feature, review the source chain and the unknowns. Proposed checks above remain unrun. Technical checks of this documentation cannot prove audience fit, demand, or completed peer review.
