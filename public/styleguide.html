00 / Direction

Decisions first.

A welcoming identity for personal style without judgment.

Audience hypotheses / not customer research

Audience

Students

Students dressing for class who want to look put together and express their style. Also consider students who want to get dressed quickly.

Archetype

Everyperson

Belonging and approachability, without telling visitors how they must dress.

Visual direction

Postmodernist

Mixed lettering, arched framing, and offset layers. The learner chose B because it felt easier to navigate.

06 / Voice

Words are
design, too.

Calm, casual, encouraging. Offer possibilities instead of rules.

Calm and welcoming

“No dress code. Make it yours.”

The learner wanted students to feel no obligation to dress in a specific way.

Helpful, without pressure

“Three starting points, not rules.” Free optional styling ideas offer practical value (reciprocity). Friendly, respectful language supports liking; neither is a claim of measured persuasion. No product facts, reviews, or urgency are invented.

A living reference. Change the shared tokens and logo, then compare this board with the storefront at the same width. Check the logo, typography, components, color roles, and voice text; sharing a stylesheet alone does not keep every example current. Save the reason for your choices in SPEC.
